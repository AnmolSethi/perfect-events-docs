Documentation / Mobile Apps

Common Rejections

The handful of reasons a ticketing/scanning app most often bounces on first submission.

"Reviewer can't get past login" (Apple, most common)

Cause: The customer app requires an account, and no working demo credentials were provided.
Fix: Create a real test account on your live/demo backend and put its email and password directly in the App Review notes field. If your backend requires email verification, either pre-verify the reviewer account or note in the review notes that it's already verified.

"App is not functional" / "app is placeholder content" (Apple)

Cause: A fresh install with no events yet, or a review account tied to an empty organizer, looks empty to a reviewer with nothing to click into.
Fix: Make sure whatever backend your submitted build points at has real, published events with images before you submit — don't submit against a totally empty install.

Missing or incomplete Data Safety / App Privacy declaration

Cause: Both stores reject a listing whose privacy declaration doesn't match what the app actually does — for example, not declaring that push notification tokens or purchase data are collected.
Fix: Be thorough and honest: account info, device/push tokens, and payment-related data (even if a payment SDK handles the sensitive part) should generally all be declared.

Camera permission rejected (scanner app)

Cause: The scanner app's camera-usage permission string is vague or missing context for why it needs the camera.
Fix: Make sure the permission description clearly explains it's for scanning ticket QR codes at check-in — edit it directly in Info.plist/AndroidManifest.xml if the default wording needs to be more specific for your brand.

Payment flow issues (Apple specifically)

Cause: Apple requires In-App Purchase for digital goods/services consumed within the app, but physical/real-world event tickets are generally treated as a "reader"-style external service and allowed to use a regular payment gateway — reviewers occasionally flag this incorrectly regardless.
Fix: If flagged, respond in Resolution Center explaining that tickets are for a real-world, in-person event (not digital content consumed in-app) — this is a well-established exception category, but worth stating plainly rather than assuming the reviewer already knows your app's specific case.

Metadata rejection (both stores)

Cause: Screenshots showing content that doesn't match the current build, or a description mentioning features not actually present.
Fix: Keep screenshots and description in sync with what you actually ship — easy to let slip after rebranding if you reuse old screenshots.
